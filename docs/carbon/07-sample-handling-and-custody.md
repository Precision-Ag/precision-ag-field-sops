# :material-package-variant: Sample Handling and Chain of Custody

Once a sample is bagged, keeping it intact and traceable through to the lab matters as
much as collecting it correctly.

!!! info "AT A GLANCE"
    Keep samples cool and out of direct sun, pack boxes to a maximum of 20kg, and get
    them freighted within 24 hours of finishing a sampling round.

## Sample packing and handling

- Keep samples out of direct sunlight and stored securely, to prevent damage to the
  bags, where possible.
- Keep samples cool where possible.
- Pack samples safely and securely into boxes for transport to the specified
  laboratory. Make sure samples cannot move during transport, which risks damaging bags
  and sample integrity.
- Sample boxes must weigh no more than 20 kilograms.

## Chain of custody

Chain of Custody (CoC) is the ability to show where a sample was at every stage, from
extraction through to receipt at the lab. It has two parts: sample freight, and
laboratory submittal documentation.

### Sample freight

Contractors are responsible for arranging freight to the AgriProve-specified
laboratory, using a reputable freight or courier service, with the CoC documented (for
example, via consignment notes).

!!! warning "WARNING"
    Freight samples as soon as possible after finishing a sampling round, and always
    within 24 hours. Provide AgriProve with the freight details (consignment notes, and
    so on) once dispatched.

### Laboratory submittals

AgriProve determines which laboratory receives the samples. Four labs are currently
used for analysis:

- EAL
- HRL
- Nutrient Advantage
- CSBP

AgriProve provides lab-specific submittal instructions to the contractor at
engagement.
